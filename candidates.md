# Candidates — One Line of Earned Pride

Judge criteria, most important first:
1. Genuinely proud — a quiet, earned moment of pride for someone in a scene.
2. Concrete scene with a real person in it, specific enough the moment is SEEN happening.
3. Pride earned inside the line by effort or difficulty the reader can feel — never asserted, congratulated or explained.
4. Lands in one read — pride arrives by the last word, no setup to assemble.
5. Freshness — an image/detail/turn not seen a hundred times; NOT trophy, podium or graduation.

Scores are 1–5 per criterion (5 = best).

## 1. The janitor (CHOSEN)
> For eleven years he mopped the same school hallway, and tonight the principal stopped, looked at the shine, and asked who kept the floors like that.

- C1: 5 — quiet, earned pride; nobody announces it, it just arrives.
- C2: 5 — a named place, a visible shine, two real people, a question asked aloud.
- C3: 5 — eleven years of unseen work is the effort; the reader feels the weight of it.
- C4: 5 — the pride lands on the last word: the principal's question is the recognition.
- C5: 5 — a mop, a shine, a hallway; no trophy, no stage, no applause.
- **Total: 25/25. 148 characters.**

## 2. The driving test
> After three winters of failing her driving test, Mrs. Alvarez parallel-parked the old sedan on the first try, cut the engine, and sat there smiling at her own hands.

- C1: 4 — earned and quiet, but "smiling at her own hands" leans slightly sentimental.
- C2: 5 — concrete car, concrete manoeuvre, a named person.
- C3: 4 — three winters of failure is felt, but the pass itself is quick.
- C4: 4 — the pride arrives near the end, but the last clause softens it.
- C5: 4 — a driving test is a familiar image, though the old sedan helps.
- **Total: 21/25. 172 characters.**

## 3. The baker's apprentice
> She had burned the first forty loaves, but this morning the baker's apprentice pulled out a crust that sang when she tapped it, and she ate the heel standing up.

- C1: 4 — earned, quiet, private; the pride is in eating the heel standing up.
- C2: 5 — a crust that sings, a heel eaten standing up; the scene is seen.
- C3: 5 — forty burned loaves is effort you can feel.
- C4: 4 — the last beat is good, but "standing up" needs a half-second of assembly.
- C5: 5 — a singing crust is a fresh, sensory detail.
- **Total: 23/25. 173 characters.**

## 4. The hill
> At five in the morning she laced her shoes for the six-hundredth run, and this time the hill that had always beaten her didn't.

- C1: 4 — earned, quiet; but the pride is mostly implied by the last word.
- C2: 3 — a hill and a pair of shoes, but the moment itself is barely seen.
- C3: 4 — six hundred mornings is effort, though it is counted rather than felt.
- C4: 5 — the pride arrives exactly on the last word: "didn't."
- C5: 4 — a morning run is a familiar image, the six-hundredth count saves it.
- **Total: 20/25. 136 characters.**

## 5. The stuttering boy
> The boy who stuttered through every school assembly read the last page of his speech without once looking down, and his mother in the third row finally breathed.

- C1: 4 — earned and moving, but the pride belongs partly to the mother, not the boy.
- C2: 5 — an assembly hall, a last page, a third row; the scene is seen.
- C3: 4 — years of stuttering is the difficulty, but it is told, not felt.
- C4: 3 — the last word is "breathed," which resolves tension rather than landing pride.
- C5: 3 — a school speech is a well-worn image.
- **Total: 19/25. 175 characters.**

## 6. The night classes
> After two years of night classes, the warehouse clerk read the forklift exam aloud to his daughter, and every answer came out in his own voice.

- C1: 4 — earned and quiet; pride arrives, but gently.
- C2: 4 — a kitchen-table scene, a daughter listening; concrete, though the exam is offstage.
- C3: 4 — two years of night classes is real effort.
- C4: 4 — "his own voice" is a good last beat, though slightly explanatory.
- C5: 4 — a forklift exam read aloud to a daughter is an unusual turn.
- **Total: 20/25. 153 characters.**

## 7. The night-shift nurse
> The night-shift nurse who had failed her anatomy exam twice threaded the IV on the first try, and the old man in bed twelve squeezed her wrist and said, "Steady hands."

- C1: 4 — earned, quiet, witnessed by another person.
- C2: 5 — an IV, a wrist, a bed number; the scene is seen.
- C3: 3 — two failed exams is stated, not felt; the success comes fast.
- C4: 3 — the last words praise her, which is close to congratulating her.
- C5: 4 — a hospital scene is common, but "Steady hands" is a fresh turn.
- **Total: 19/25. 185 characters.**

## Winner
Candidate 1, the janitor — the only line scoring 5 on every criterion. Revised once
in this session (see main.ts history): the opening was tightened from
"He had mopped the same school hallway for eleven years, and tonight …"
to "For eleven years he mopped the same school hallway, and tonight …",
keeping the same last word, the principal's question, where the pride lands.
